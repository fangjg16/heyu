# Research Principles

These rules apply to every enrichment wave.

## Iterative search

Each topic gets several rounds, not one query:

1. Broad overview
2. Drill-down on what round 1 found
3. Cross-check a second independent source
4. Reality check: disconfirming evidence, failures, and edge cases

Stop only after at least three query variations, or when the gap is explicit.

## Source quality

Map findings onto `references/shared/source-grading.md`. In narrative notes you may also use:

| Screening note | Typical sources |
|---|---|
| Strong public anchor | regulators, registries, courts, audited filings, established industry databases |
| Usable secondary | established press, trade publications, sourced research |
| Weak / colour | blogs, social posts, unsourced pages, marketing sites |

A marketing page repeating the target’s own claim is the same authoring party. Do not count it as corroboration.

## Cross-reference

For every finding that will move a dimension score, look for a second independent source. If sources agree, cite both. If they disagree, keep both and say so.

## Quantification

Prefer dated numbers with definition, geography, and source ID. “The market is growing” is not a finding.

## Dating

Record publication date and the date of the underlying fact. Flag material older than 18 months when the category moves quickly.

## Search failures

1. Rephrase at least three times before declaring a gap.
2. A proxy is allowed if the formula and the weaker fit are visible.
3. Write the gap: what was sought, what was tried, closest proxy if any.
4. Do not fabricate.
5. If the missing fact is something only the target can supply, send it to open questions. If it was a public fact this skill should have found, it is an agent gap, not a target gap.

## Independence

The user’s description and any target deck are one perspective. Public enrichment exists to test that perspective, not to paraphrase it.
