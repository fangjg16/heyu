# Public Enrichment

Use in Phase 3. Produce `02-screening/enrichment.md`. The question for every wave is the same: does public evidence support a real investment opportunity in this project now?

Run the four waves in order. Pass a short finding summary into the next wave. Cover every wave. Screening depth is enough to score and to write open questions; it is not formal industry, business, or financial diligence.

Search in the same run. Record source IDs in `02-evidence/source-register.md`. Reuse existing IDs.

Identity, control, affiliation, or adverse-record signals that survive a name check belong in the team/founder notes and, when material, on the list for `due-diligence` Phase 3B. Team scoring itself is Phase 4.

## Wave 1 — Market landscape and timing

Ask whether demand is real, whether the window is open, and whether regulation is an immediate hard stop.

Search for: category size and growth with definitions; capital and M&A temperature; technology or channel inflections; precedent public failures; licences or rules in the stated geography.

Write:

- demand: who pays, from which budget, how often
- timing: opening, open, overheating, already passed, or not yet formed
- regulation: visible blocker, watch item, or none found, with coverage limits
- what this means for whether diligence is worth starting now
- gaps

A hot narrative is not `meets` on timing. A structurally closed or unformed window can support `misses` later.

Do not build a full TAM/SAM/SOM triangle or an industry workpaper.

## Wave 2 — Competitive alternatives and position

Ask what customers buy today instead, and whether the target has a position that public facts can support.

Search for: named direct players; traditional workarounds; in-house build; doing nothing; category leaders; public positioning and obvious saturation.

Write:

- alternatives the customer actually uses
- the target’s claimed difference, tested against public facts
- whether “no competitors” survives this wave
- what this means for competitive positioning
- gaps

Do not produce battle cards or a full pricing landscape.

## Wave 3 — Customer and demand evidence

Ask whether anyone is paying, publicly looking for a solution, or describing the pain in their own words, and whether that matches the intake customer.

Search for: reviews, forums, tender notices, hiring that implies delivery, trade-press customer stories, complaints about current solutions.

Write:

- evidence of willingness to pay or active search
- mismatch between the stated customer and public voices
- demand signals that confirm or weaken Wave 1 timing
- gaps

Do not run customer interviews. Absence of public chatter is a gap or a caution, not automatic `misses`.

## Wave 4 — Distribution and market entry

Ask whether the business can reach the customers it claims, in the geography it claims.

Search for: how similar offerings are sold; channel concentration; partnership or platform dependence; geographic access, licences, or local incumbents.

Write:

- plausible paths to the stated customer
- paths that exist only in the narrative
- geographic or channel constraints
- gaps

Do not design a go-to-market plan.

## Synthesis inside enrichment.md

After the four waves, write a short roll-up:

- what public evidence supports an opportunity now
- what contradicts the description or deck
- what remains unknown
- implications for each scoring dimension

Conflicts stay visible. Do not pick the convenient side.
